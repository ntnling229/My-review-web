§ 9.1 Relations and Their Properties (關係及其性質)

1. Introduction & Basic Definitions (基本定義與表示法)

Definition: Binary Relation (二元關係) (page 573)

Let $A$ and $B$ be two sets. A binary relation (二元關係) from $A$ to $B$ is a subset of $A \times B$.

Notation: $a \, R \, b \iff (a,b) \in R$

If $(a,b) \in R$, $a$ is said to be related to $b$ by $R$. One subset one relation.

Example 1: $A =$ the set of students in your school, $B =$ the set of courses, $R =$ relation where $(a,b) \in R \iff$ $a$ is a student enrolled in course $b$.

Example 2: $A =$ the set of all cities, $B =$ the set of 50 states in USA, $R =$ relation where $(a,b) \in R \iff$ city $a$ is in state $b$.

Example 3: $A = \{0, 1, 2\}$, $B = \{a, b\}$, $R = \{(0,a), (0,b), (1,a), (2,b)\}$.

Question & Formula: Number of Relations (關係總數)

If $|A| = m$, $|B| = n$, how many relations are there from $A$ to $B$?

Answer: $2^{m \times n}$

Why? Because $|A \times B| = m \times n$, and a relation is a subset of $A \times B$. The number of subsets of a set with $k$ elements is $2^k$.

2. Functions as Relations (函數作為關係)

From Functions to Relations

A function $f$ from $A$ to $B$ assigns exactly one element of $B$ to each element of $A$.

The graph of $f$ is the set of ordered pairs $(a,b)$, where $b = f(a)$.

Properties: Every element of $A$ is the first element of exactly one ordered pair of the graph.

Comparison & One-to-Many

If $R$ is a relation from $A$ to $B$ such that every element in $A$ is the first element of exactly one ordered pair of $R$, then a function $f(a)=b$ can be defined with $R$ as its graph.

• A relation can express a one-to-many (一對多) relationship.
• A function represents a relation where exactly one element of $B$ is related to each element of $A$.

4. Properties of Relations (關係的四大性質)

(1) Reflexive Relation (自反關係) (page 576)
$(a,a) \in R$ for every element $a \in A$.

所有元素都有到自身的自環 (self-loop)。對角線元素全為 1。

(2a) Symmetric Relation (對稱關係) (page 577)
$(b,a) \in R$ whenever $(a,b) \in R$, for all $a,b \in A$.

若有由 $a$ 到 $b$ 的邊，必有由 $b$ 到 $a$ 的邊。

(2b) Antisymmetric Relation (反對稱關係) (page 577)
$(a,b) \in R$ and $(b,a) \in R \implies a = b$, for all $a,b \in A$.

若 $a \ne b$，則 $(a,b)$ 與 $(b,a)$ 不可能同時存在於 $R$ 中。

(3) Transitive Relation (遞移關係) (page 578)
$(a,b) \in R$ and $(b,c) \in R \implies (a,c) \in R$, for all $a,b,c \in A$.

有路徑長度 2 的起終點必有長度 1 的直接邊。

Relations on $\{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ (Example 7 & 10 & 13):
  • $R_1 = \{(1,1), (1,2), (2,1), (2,2), (3,4), (4,1), (4,4)\}$
  • $R_2 = \{(1,1), (1,2), (2,1)\}$
  • $R_3 = \{(1,1), (1,2), (1,4), (2,1), (2,2), (3,2), (3,3), (4,1), (4,4)\}$
  • $R_4 = \{(2,1), (3,1), (3,2), (4,1), (4,2), (4,3)\}$
  • $R_5 = \{(1,1), (1,2), (1,3), (1,4), (2,2), (2,3), (2,4), (3,3), (3,4), (4,4)\}$
  • $R_6 = \{(3,4)\}$
Reflexive: $R_3, R_5$
Symmetric: $R_2, R_3$

Integer Relations Analysis (Example 5 & 11 & 14):
Reflexive: $R_1 (\le), R_3 (= \text{or} -), R_4 (=)$
Symmetric: $R_3, R_4, R_6 (a+b \le 3)$

Divides relation ($\mid$) on positive integers (Example 12 & 15):

• Reflexive: Yes ($a \mid a$).

• Symmetric: No ($1 \mid 2$ but $2 \nmid 1$).

• Antisymmetric: Yes (if $a \mid b$ and $b \mid a$, then $a = b$ for positive integers).

• Transitive: Yes (if $a \mid b$ and $b \mid c$, then $a \mid c$).

Question (Example 16): How many reflexive relations are there on a set with $n$ elements?

Answer: $2^{n(n-1)}$

Reason: In an $n \times n$ matrix, the $n$ diagonal elements $(a,a)$ MUST be 1. The remaining $n^2 - n = n(n-1)$ entries can be either 0 or 1, yielding $2^{n(n-1)}$ choices.

5. Combining Relations, Composite & Power (關係的組合、複合與次數)

Combining Relations (集合運算組合)

Since relations $R_1, R_2 \subseteq A \times B$ are sets, we can combine them using standard set operations: $R_1 \cup R_2$, $R_1 \cap R_2$, $R_1 - R_2$, $R_2 - R_1$, $R_1 \oplus R_2$.

Example 17 (page 579): $A=\{1,2,3\}$, $B=\{1,2,3,4\}$, $R_1 = \{(1,1), (2,2), (3,3)\}$, $R_2 = \{(1,1), (1,2), (1,3)\}$.

  • $R_1 \cup R_2 = \{(1,1), (1,2), (1,3), (2,2), (3,3)\}$
  • $R_1 \cap R_2 = \{(1,1)\}$
  • $R_1 - R_2 = \{(2,2), (3,3)\}$
  • $R_2 - R_1 = \{(1,2), (1,3)\}$

Example 18 (page 579): $R_1 = \{(a,b) \mid a \text{ has taken course } b\}$, $R_2 = \{(a,b) \mid a \text{ requires course } b \text{ to graduate}\}$.

  • • $R_1 \cup R_2$: student $a$ has taken course $b$ OR requires course $b$ to graduate.
  • • $R_1 \cap R_2$: student $a$ has taken course $b$ AND requires course $b$ to graduate.
  • • $R_1 - R_2$: student $a$ has taken course $b$ BUT course $b$ is NOT required for graduation.
  • • $R_2 - R_1$: student $a$ requires course $b$ to graduate BUT has NOT taken it yet.

Example 19: $R_1 = \{(x,y) \mid x < y\}$, $R_2 = \{(x,y) \mid x > y\}$.

• $R_1 \cup R_2 = \{(x,y) \mid x \ne y\}$, $R_1 \cap R_2 = \emptyset$, $R_1 \oplus R_2 = \{(x,y) \mid x \ne y\}$, $R_1 - R_2 = R_1$, $R_2 - R_1 = R_2$.

Definition 6: Composite of Two Relations (關係的複合) (page 580)

Let $R$ be a relation from set $A$ to set $B$ and $S$ a relation from $B$ to $C$. The composite of $R$ and $S$ is the relation consisting of ordered pairs $(a,c)$, where $a \in A$, $c \in C$, and for which there exists an element $b \in B$ such that $(a,b) \in R$ and $(b,c) \in S$.

Notation: $S \circ R$

$R$ from $\{1,2,3\}$ to $\{1,2,3,4\}$ with $R = \{(1,1), (1,4), (2,3), (3,1), (3,4)\}$.

$S$ from $\{1,2,3,4\}$ to $\{0,1,2\}$ with $S = \{(1,0), (2,0), (3,1), (3,2), (4,1)\}$.

$S \circ R = \{(1,0), (1,1), (2,1), (2,2), (3,0), (3,1)\}$

Definition 7: Power of a Relation (關係的次數) (page 580)

Let $R$ be a relation on the set $A$. The powers $R^n$, $n=1,2,3,\dots$ are defined recursively by:

$R^1 = R \quad \text{and} \quad R^{n+1} = R^n \circ R$

Let $R = \{(1,1), (2,1), (3,2), (4,3)\}$.

• $R^2 = R \circ R = \{(1,1), (2,1), (3,1), (4,2)\}$

• $R^3 = R^2 \circ R = \{(1,1), (2,1), (3,1), (4,1)\}$

• $R^4 = R^3 \circ R = \{(1,1), (2,1), (3,1), (4,1)\}$

• For $n \ge 3$, $R^n = R^3$.

Theorem 1: Characterization of Transitivity by Powers (遞移性定理)

The relation $R$ on a set $A$ is transitive if and only if $R^n \subseteq R$ for $n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$

Part 1: Proof of "If" part ($\impliedby$)

Suppose that $R^n \subseteq R$ for $n=1,2,\dots$. In particular, $R^2 \subseteq R$.

To see that this implies $R$ is transitive, note that if $(a,b) \in R$ and $(b,c) \in R$, then by definition of composite, $(a,c) \in R^2$. Because $R^2 \subseteq R$, this means $(a,c) \in R$. Hence, $R$ is transitive.

Part 2: Proof of "Only If" part ($\implies$) using Mathematical Induction

Assume $R$ is transitive. We prove $R^n \subseteq R$ for all $n \ge 1$ by induction on $n$.

  • Basis Step ($n=1$): $R^1 = R \subseteq R$ is trivially true.
  • Inductive Hypothesis: Suppose $R^n \subseteq R$ holds for a positive integer $n$.
  • Inductive Step: Consider any $(a,b) \in R^{n+1} = R^n \circ R$. By definition of composition, there exists $x \in A$ such that $(a,x) \in R$ and $(x,b) \in R^n$.
    By the inductive hypothesis $R^n \subseteq R$, so $(x,b) \in R$.
    Since $R$ is transitive, $(a,x) \in R$ and $(x,b) \in R$ implies $(a,b) \in R$.
    This proves $R^{n+1} \subseteq R$.

By mathematical induction, $R^n \subseteq R$ for all $n \ge 1$. $\blacksquare$

§ 9.2 n-ary Relations and Their Applications ($n$ 元關係及其應用)

Definition 1: n-ary Relation ($n$ 元關係) (page 583)

Let $A_1, A_2, \dots, A_n$ be sets. An n-ary relation on these sets is a subset of

$A_1 \times A_2 \times \dots \times A_n$

  • The sets $A_1, A_2, \dots, A_n$ are called the domains (定義域) of the relation.
  • $n$ is called its degree (度數).

Example 1: Let $R$ be the relation on $\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N}$ consisting of triples $(a,b,c)$ where $a,b,c$ are integers with $a < b < c$.

• $(1,2,3) \in R$, but $(2,4,3) \notin R$.

Example 4 (page 584): Let $R$ be the relation consisting of 5-tuples $(A,N,S,D,T)$ representing airplane flights, where $A$ is the airline, $N$ is the flight number, $S$ is the starting point, $D$ is the destination, and $T$ is the departure time.

• $(\text{Nadir}, 963, \text{Newark}, \text{Bangor}, 15:00) \in R$.

§ 9.4 Closures of Relations (關係的閉包)

1. Introduction & General Definition (閉包通用定義)

The Closure of Relation $R$ with Respect to Property $P$ (page 597)

Let $R$ be a relation on $A$. $R$ may or may not have some property $P$ (such as reflexive, symmetric, or transitive).

If there is a relation $S$ with property $P$ containing $R$ ($R \subseteq S$) such that $S$ is a subset of every relation with property $P$ containing $R$, then $S$ is called the closure of $R$ with respect to $P$ (關於性質 $P$ 的閉包).

2. Reflexive & Symmetric Closures (自反閉包與對稱閉包)

Reflexive Closure (自反閉包)

Let $\Delta = \{(a,a) \mid a \in A\}$ be the diagonal relation (對角線關係).

Reflexive Closure $= R \cup \Delta$

Example 1: $R = \{(a,b) \mid a < b\}$ on integers $\implies$ Reflexive closure $= R \cup \Delta = \{(a,b) \mid a \le b\}$.

Symmetric Closure (對稱閉包)

Let $R^{-1} = \{(b,a) \mid (a,b) \in R\}$ be the inverse relation.

Symmetric Closure $= R \cup R^{-1}$

Example 2: $R = \{(a,b) \mid a > b\}$ on positive integers $\implies$ Symmetric closure $= R \cup R^{-1} = \{(a,b) \mid a \ne b\}$.

Let $S = R \cup R^{-1}$. We must prove three conditions:

1) Prove $S$ is symmetric:

For any $(a,b) \in S = R \cup R^{-1}$, there are two cases:

i) $(a,b) \in R \implies (b,a) \in R^{-1} \implies (b,a) \in S$.

ii) $(a,b) \in R^{-1} \implies (b,a) \in R \implies (b,a) \in S$. Thus $S$ is symmetric.

2) Prove $R \subseteq S$:

Obvious from $S = R \cup R^{-1}$.

3) Prove $S$ is the smallest such relation ($S \subseteq T$ for any symmetric $T \supseteq R$):

Suppose $T$ is symmetric and $R \subseteq T$. Let $(a,b) \in S = R \cup R^{-1}$.

i) If $(a,b) \in R$, then $(a,b) \in T$ because $R \subseteq T$.

ii) If $(a,b) \in R^{-1}$, then $(b,a) \in R \subseteq T$. Since $T$ is symmetric, $(b,a) \in T \implies (a,b) \in T$.

In both cases $(a,b) \in T$. Thus $S \subseteq T$. $\blacksquare$

3. Paths in Directed Graphs & Theorem 1 (有向圖路徑與定理)

Definition 1: Path & Circuit (路徑與迴路) (page 599)

A path (路徑) from $a$ to $b$ in directed graph $G$ is a sequence of edges $(x_0, x_1), (x_1, x_2), \dots, (x_{n-1}, x_n)$ where $n$ is a nonnegative integer, $x_0 = a$, $x_n = b$. Denoted by $x_0, x_1, \dots, x_n$ with length $n$.

A path of length $n \ge 1$ that begins and ends at the same vertex is called a circuit / cycle (迴路).

Example 3 Path Questions:

• $a, b, e, d$: Path of length 3.

• $a, e, c, d, b$: Not a path (no edge from $c$ to $d$).

Theorem 1 (Path Theorem) (page 600)

Let $R$ be a relation on a set $A$. There is a path of length $n$ ($n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$) from $a$ to $b$ if and only if $(a,b) \in R^n$.

4. Transitive Closure & Connectivity Relation (遞移閉包與連通關係)

Definition 2: Connectivity Relation $R^*$ (連通關係) (page 600)

Let $R$ be a relation on a set $A$. The connectivity relation $R^*$ consists of the pairs $(a,b)$ such that there is a path of length at least one from $a$ to $b$ in $R$.

$R^* = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} R^n$

Theorem 2: Transitive Closure Theorem (page 601)

The transitive closure of a relation $R$ equals the connectivity relation $R^*$.

1) $R^*$ contains $R$: $R^1 = R \subseteq R^*$ by definition.

2) $R^*$ is transitive: If $(a,b) \in R^*$ and $(b,c) \in R^*$, there exist paths from $a$ to $b$ and $b$ to $c$. Combining them gives a path from $a$ to $c$, so $(a,c) \in R^*$. Thus $R^*$ is transitive.

3) $R^*$ is the smallest transitive relation containing $R$: Suppose $S$ is any transitive relation containing $R$ ($R \subseteq S$). Since $S$ is transitive, $S^n \subseteq S$ for all $n \ge 1$ (by Theorem 1 of Section 9.1). It follows that $S^* \subseteq S$. Since $R \subseteq S$, any path in $R$ is a path in $S$, so $R^* \subseteq S^* \subseteq S$. Consequently, $R^* \subseteq S$. $\blacksquare$

Lemma 1 (Path Length Bound) (page 601)

Let $A$ be a set with $n$ elements, and $R$ be a relation on $A$. If there is a path of length at least one in $R$ from $a$ to $b$, then there is a path with length not exceeding $n$.

Moreover, when $a \ne b$, if there is a path from $a$ to $b$, then there is such a path with length not exceeding $n-1$.

FIGURE 2: Producing a Path with Length Not Exceeding $n$ (原教材圖形重現)

From Lemma 1, the transitive closure $R^*$ of a relation $R$ on a set with $n$ elements is the finite union:

$R^* = R \cup R^2 \cup R^3 \cup \dots \cup R^{n-1}$ if $n > 1$
$R^* = R \cup \phantom{R^2 \cup R^3 \cup \dots \cup R^{n-1}}$ if $n = 1$
$R^* = R \cup R^2 \cup R^3 \cup \dots \cup R^n$ if $n \ge 1$

§ 9.5 Equivalence Relations (等價關係)

1. Definition of Equivalence Relation (等價關係定義)

Definition 1: Equivalence Relation (page 608)

A relation $R$ on a set $A$ is called an equivalence relation (等價關係) if it is:

1. Reflexive (自反)
2. Symmetric (對稱)
3. Transitive (遞移)

Remark: Two elements $a,b$ that are related by an equivalence relation are called equivalent (等價), written $a \sim b$ or $a \, R \, b$.

(a) String Length Relation: $a \, R \, b \iff l(a) = l(b)$ on English strings.
Reflexive: $l(a)=l(a)$; Symmetric: $l(a)=l(b) \implies l(b)=l(a)$; Transitive: $l(a)=l(b) \land l(b)=l(c) \implies l(a)=l(c)$. $\implies$ Equivalence relation.
(b) Example 2 (page 608): $a \, R \, b \iff a = b \text{ or } a = -b \iff |a| = |b|$ on integers $\mathbb{Z}$.
Reflexive, Symmetric, Transitive. $\implies$ Equivalence relation.
(c) Example 3: $a \, R \, b \iff a - b \in \mathbb{Z}$ on real numbers $\mathbb{R}$.
1) $a - a = 0 \in \mathbb{Z} \implies a \, R \, a$.
2) If $a - b = k \in \mathbb{Z}$, then $b - a = -k \in \mathbb{Z} \implies b \, R \, a$.
3) If $a - b = k_1 \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $b - c = k_2 \in \mathbb{Z}$, then $a - c = (a-b) + (b-c) = k_1 + k_2 \in \mathbb{Z} \implies a \, R \, c$. $\implies$ Equivalence relation.
(d) Congruence Modulo $m$ (模 $m$ 同餘) (page 608):
Let $m > 1$ be an integer. Relation $R = \{(a,b) \mid a \equiv b \pmod m\} \iff m \mid (a - b)$.
This divides all integers into $m$ distinct congruence classes. Is an equivalence relation.

2. Equivalence Classes (等價類)

Definition 2: Equivalence Class (page 610)

Let $R$ be an equivalence relation on a set $A$. The set of all elements that are related to an element $a$ of $A$ is called the equivalence class (等價類) of $a$.

$[a]_R = \{s \mid (a,s) \in R\}$

Example 8 (page 610): For $a \, R \, b \iff |a| = |b|$, $[a] = \{-a, a\}$.

• $[7] = \{-7, 7\}$, $[5] = \{-5, 5\}$, $[0] = \{0\}$.

Congruence Modulo $m$ Equivalence Classes:

$[a]_m = \{\dots, a-2m, a-m, a, a+m, a+2m, \dots\}$

For modulo 4:

• $[0]_4 = \{\dots, -8, -4, 0, 4, 8, \dots\}$

• $[1]_4 = \{\dots, -7, -3, 1, 5, 9, \dots\}$

• $[2]_4 = \{\dots, -6, -2, 2, 6, 10, \dots\}$

• $[3]_4 = \{\dots, -5, -1, 3, 7, 11, \dots\}$

3. Equivalence Classes & Partition Theorems (等價類與分割定理)

Theorem 1: Characterization of Equivalence Classes (page 612)

Let $R$ be an equivalence relation on a set $A$. These statements are equivalent:

(i) $a \, R \, b$
(ii) $[a] = [b]$
(iii) $[a] \cap [b] \ne \emptyset$
(i) implies (ii): Suppose $a \, R \, b$. For any $c \in [a]$, we have $a \, R \, c$. Since $a \, R \, b$ and $R$ is symmetric, $b \, R \, a$. Since $R$ is transitive, $b \, R \, a$ and $a \, R \, c \implies b \, R \, c$, so $c \in [b]$. This proves $[a] \subseteq [b]$. Similarly, $[b] \subseteq [a]$. Therefore $[a] = [b]$.
(ii) implies (iii): Assume $[a] = [b]$. Since $R$ is reflexive, $a \in [a]$. Thus $a \in [a] \cap [b]$, so $[a] \cap [b] \ne \emptyset$.
(iii) implies (i): Suppose $[a] \cap [b] \ne \emptyset$. Then there exists $c \in [a] \cap [b]$, meaning $a \, R \, c$ and $b \, R \, c$. By symmetry, $c \, R \, b$. By transitivity, $a \, R \, c$ and $c \, R \, b \implies a \, R \, b$. $\blacksquare$
Partition of a Set (集合的分割) (page 613)

A partition (分割) of a set $S$ is a collection of disjoint nonempty subsets $A_i$ ($i \in I$) of $S$ that have $S$ as their union:

  • 1. $A_i \ne \emptyset$ for all $i \in I$
  • 2. $A_i \cap A_j = \emptyset$ when $i \ne j$ (Disjoint)
  • 3. $\bigcup_{i \in I} A_i = S$

Example 7 (page 613): Let $S = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$. $A_1 = \{1,2,3\}$, $A_2 = \{4,5\}$, $A_3 = \{6\}$ forms a partition of $S$.

Constructing Relation from Partition: $a \, R \, b \iff a$ and $b$ are in the same subset.

• Ordered pairs: $A_1 \times A_1 \cup A_2 \times A_2 \cup A_3 \times A_3$.

Theorem 2: Fundamental Theorem of Equivalence Relations (page 613)

Let $R$ be an equivalence relation on a set $S$. Then the equivalence classes of $R$ form a partition of $S$.

Conversely, given a partition $\{A_i \mid i \in I\}$ of the set $S$, there is an equivalence relation $R$ that has the sets $A_i$ as its equivalence classes.
